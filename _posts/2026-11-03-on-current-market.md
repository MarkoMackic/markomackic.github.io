In the world where everything is loud, a quite place is a great privillege.

I don't know where to start, this is more of a personal rant than any kind of valuable information, and
I really suck at communicating those perspectives effectively.

In the last year I had so much time available to reflect on my personal and professional life, I resigned a job, closed a company, and
went on doing my hobbies not related to tech during that time. I also tried to start up some bbullshit stuff and it didn't work
out at the end. So we're here, life is life, and looking past it, all the processes that have happened were fun and enlightening.

Looking at the tech scene at my country, it's not looking well, there were many layoffs and the companies still standing are
like really struggling to keep the clients, or they have some internal products they developed and now live off of that, but most
of our tech scene was based on outsourcing. ALl of the sudden, the demand for that got way lower, and many are suffering.

So economy of beeing a "software guy" is suddenly starting to decline, and like it was to be expected. 
Many people jumped into tech because it's like "yo, we gonna be rich" and than spilling the knowledge about optimal
way to change button color on a website. I had to delete my social networks, specially those coined as "professional".
Can't really stand to hear simple things wrapped and presented to raise value of an individual.

Anyways, people were payed very well for chaning button colors, and no one seemed to care about deeper knowledge in
terms of profession they're in, just cared to be good enough. Now we have a different situation, what was good enough
is not good enough anymore.

AI has entered the game, and now we have many hypers who believe AI is going to solve all of our problems, and on 
the other side there are those who fear for their lives, because AI can take their jobs away. What makes the second
class more worried is the AI already has shown some potential, like it is useful to an extent where it can bring
meaningful improvements to our day-to-day work. Sofware people use AI, MDs use AI, car mechanics use AI and regular people use AI
in everyday life.

Like I had some conversations with non tech people, and when I ask them somethin, they like "ask chat", I'm like lol.

We can certianly use AI to improve our day to day workflow so we can be more productive, and I do agree on doing that where
you already know how you'd do it without the tool, but I do not and will not ever be agreeing upon blind usage of anything however helpful that may be at the moment. 

In my career I had a privillege to mentor a couple of student interns and also to witness how people choose shortcuts 
even thought they help em nothing. I had an intern who submitted PRs without understanding any single change it does, where he'd
just go paste issue from JIRA to Copilot, and submit the result as PR. He could not explain any change whatsoever. I would never
want that type of people with me anywhere on anything I do.
So AI without understanding why is a certian no, AI to reduce repetition is a certian yes would be the conclusion of
my opionion about it.

To also note, it's certianly going to be more of a thing in the years to come, at least we can expect that having in mind
current tech industry trajectory, more data centers, investments in accelerators, new models ..
But I would not like to see a society where tech becomes our arms and legs, or brain for that matter.
It's very useful to have a calculator, but to not know division using one is absurd.

I expect huge changes in the market in the years to come. There are couple disturbances that are shaking and sieving
our current way of life. Some people will not feel any of it, some people will feel it drastically, maybe it even
changes the course of their lives. The "useless" jobs are going to lower in the years to come, since economy hits 
companies as hard. Number of highly educated people is probably going to increase as it's going to give those
individuals higher chance of success, and I also think people are going to learn harder since as stated "good enough" has shifted somewhat. The number of "work force" outside intellectual I assume is not going to change too rapidly, neither up or down.

Whatever the market state, or anything happening around us, we should do our work with as deep of an understanding as we are able to obtain, since understanding and learning allows for adjustment to circumstances.

As for me, I'm gonna try to stay in software for the time being, and if that doesn't work out, something else will.
